More about me

My lived experience of ADHD informs my practice.

Consultant and coach working at the intersection of gender identity and ADHD — one conversation, one workshop, one talk at a time.

My background and values

I am an ADHD coach and expert on gender, diversity and inclusion. My coaching work sits at the intersection of two things I care deeply about, women's empowerment and ADHD. I have lived experience of the interplay between ADHD and hormones and being diagnosed later in life and of grappling with ADHD along with perimenopause.

I have worked in the gender, diversity, equality and inclusion space for 24 years, and my own ADHD journey has meant that I include neurodiversity as part of my gender, diversity and inclusion work in a more intentional way.

I was diagnosed with ADHD at age 42 and have since learned a lot about ADHD, Autism and the interplay between hormones and ADHD (and the gender dimensions of neurodiversity, especially regarding diagnosis, differentiated treatment and social expectations and medical/pharmaceutical research).

Having studied human rights and social anthropology, I bring an intersectional, human-centred approach, one which focuses on the inherent dignity of the human being (and the uniqueness of each person which deserves to be celebrated). I also bring an inter-cultural sensitivity, honed from my time living abroad in 12 countries on 5 continents and my work in over 54 countries across Asia-Pacific, Africa, the Middle East and the Americas.

I have Masters degrees in Human Rights and Social Anthropology, and have completed the University of Oxford's Advanced Coaching course and the Co-Active Training Institute's (ICF accredited) "Fundamentals of Coaching."
